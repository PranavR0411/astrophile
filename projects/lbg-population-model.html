A photometric population model for Lyman break galaxies

Building a data-driven, SED-based population model for galaxies at z > 4, where current models such as pop-cosmos lose coverage.

Dates

Data

I am in the second year of the MSc in Physics with Extended Research at Imperial College London. Most UK master’s degrees last one year; this programme runs for two, with the whole second year dedicated to a thesis. I chose it for exactly that reason: a long, uninterrupted stretch on a single research question.

The problem

My thesis, with Dr. Boris Leistedt, builds photometric models of Lyman break galaxies (LBGs). The pop-cosmos framework is the most advanced galaxy population model available, but it lacks coverage at redshift z > 4 because of the data it is trained on, and its underlying SPS SED model is not trivial to extend. Training on deeper catalogues such as COSMOS2025 would not add enough sky area to calibrate a model suitable for LBGs as they are detected in HSC and Rubin.

So I am building a photometric model of the LBG population in the spirit of pop-cosmos, but with a more flexible model for the SEDs and their parameters. The model will be fit to multi-band data and used to predict the LBGs that should be observed in these fields.

Approach so far

I started in July 2025. After reading on LBGs and how pop-cosmos is constructed, I normalised the EAZY SED templates and decomposed them with non-negative matrix factorisation (NMF). NMF is similar to PCA but constrains the components to be non-negative, which suits SEDs, where negative flux is unphysical.

Next I needed a model that predicts the coefficients of these components to generate SED templates. For this I implemented Extreme Deconvolution (Bovy et al. 2009) from the paper rather than using an existing package, because later stages require modifying the algorithm. With the base algorithm working, I am adding the effects of redshift, dust and IGM attenuation, currently through an XD-based EM algorithm that handles redshift. The finished model will generate realistic SED templates from which galaxy properties can be predicted.
