Far-ultraviolet emission from novae in the Magellanic Clouds

Identifying optical novae in archival AstroSat/UVIT images of the SMC and LMC and measuring their far-UV fluxes.

Type
Research internship
Dates
Jun 2024 – Aug 2024
Where
Indian Institute of Astrophysics, Bengaluru
With
Supervised by Dr. Sudhanshu Barve
Status
Completed
Data
AstroSat/UVIT archival imaging (17 fields); GALEX FUV for validation
Methods
CCD reduction in CCDLab, WCS alignment, cross-matching, aperture photometry (astropy, photutils)

In the summer of 2024, just before my master’s, I spent two months at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics with Dr. Sudhanshu Barve, studying far-ultraviolet (FUV) emission from novae in the Small and Large Magellanic Clouds using archival data from the UV Imaging Telescope (UVIT) aboard ISRO’s AstroSat.

I downloaded UVIT observations of 17 fields across the two galaxies and processed them in CCDLab, applying standard CCD reduction, distortion correction and WCS alignment to produce science-ready images. I then mapped the reported coordinates of known optical novae onto the UVIT fields in SAO DS9.

Because UVIT coverage of the Magellanic Clouds is patchy, only four novae from the optical catalogue fell within the pointings. I verified each against GALEX FUV images via SIMBAD, checking crowding and the local stellar distribution to confirm the UV detections matched the same sources.

I performed FUV photometry on each nova with astropy and photutils, estimating local backgrounds in annular regions, subtracting diffuse emission and measuring integrated fluxes. This gave initial constraints on their UV brightness in outburst and indicated which novae are bright enough for multi-wavelength follow-up.

The project was short by design, but it gave me practical experience with archival data processing, background subtraction, WCS alignment, multi-mission cross-validation and UV photometry: skills that carried directly into my JWST work and master’s thesis.
