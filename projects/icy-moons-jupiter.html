As part of my master’s coursework I completed a self-directed research project on the exospheres and ionospheres of Europa, Ganymede and Callisto, reading and synthesising nearly 100 papers across planetary science, space plasma physics, magnetospheric interactions and atmospheric chemistry.

The goal was to explain the physical and chemical processes that create and sustain these extremely thin atmospheres, and how each moon’s environment shapes them. Each experiences a different balance of radiation, magnetospheric plasma, sublimation, chemistry and ionospheric processes, and the evidence is scattered across decades of missions and models.

Exospheres

  • Sputtering by energetic ions
  • Radiolysis of surface ice, producing O2, H2 and related species
  • Sublimation of H2O ice in sunlit regions
  • Ballistic transport, thermal desorption and surface recycling
  • Hemispheric asymmetry from tidal locking and magnetospheric flow

Ionospheres

  • Photoionisation by solar UV
  • Electron-impact ionisation from magnetospheric plasma
  • Charge-exchange reactions
  • Chemistry among H2O, O2, OH and their ions

Three moons compared

Europa is dominated by intense sputtering and radiolysis, producing a dense O2 exosphere and a highly variable ionosphere shaped by the Jovian environment. Evidence for plumes and asymmetric O2 distributions helps explain its unusual auroral patterns.

Ganymede has its own intrinsic magnetic field, a miniature magnetosphere within Jupiter’s. Open- and closed-field regions change where sputtering happens, which molecules accumulate and how the ionosphere is structured: sublimation dominates at low latitudes, sputtering near the poles.

Callisto, farther from Jupiter, sees weaker plasma bombardment and is dominated by sublimation and radiolysis. Its O2 and H2 exosphere is more stable and surface-bound, with a weak ionosphere detectable only when the trailing hemisphere is sunlit.

The review compares surface compositions, volatile release mechanisms, scale heights, ion species, electron densities and links to magnetospheric structure, and closes with how Europa Clipper and JUICE will transform these studies.
