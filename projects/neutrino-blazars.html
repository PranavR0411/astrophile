Gamma-ray variability of neutrino-candidate blazars

Building optimally binned Fermi-LAT light curves to test whether blazars associated with IceCube neutrinos flare differently.

Type

Dates

Where

Data

During my 2023 exchange semester at the Technical University of Munich, I took a seminar on neutrino astronomy with Dr. Elisa Resconi, a leading figure in the IceCube Collaboration. After my seminar presentation on one of her group’s IceCube papers, I asked about a research project. She invited me to a group meeting at the European Southern Observatory the next day, my first exposure to multi-messenger workflows, and offered me a project with her postdoc, Dr. Martina Karl.

The question

The project focused on blazars associated with IceCube neutrino detections, and whether these “neutrino blazars” show different gamma-ray variability from the general blazar population. Only about a third of the sample are likely true neutrino sources, so distinct variability could give physical clues to how and where neutrinos are produced in jets. I learned to extract Fermi-LAT fluxes with fermipy and to assemble SEDs and light curves for each source.

In Karl, Padovani & Giommi (2023), the variability comparison uses fractional variabilities from 4FGL-DR3, which are derived from yearly-binned light curves. Any flare on week-to-month timescales is washed out by such coarse binning. If neutrino production correlates with rapid flaring, yearly bins would miss exactly the behaviour we needed to measure.

Adaptive binning

My main task was to build the binning infrastructure. I wrote a function that, for any 4FGL source, computes the minimum time-bin length needed to reach a target detection significance, TStarget, in each bin. It uses the band-wise test-statistic contributions in the catalogue: for a chosen minimum energy, an energy-weight vector selects the LAT bands above that threshold, and the expected total TS is

TStotal = Σbands TSband × wband

Using standard LAT sensitivity scaling, the required bin length is

tbin ∝ (TStarget / TStotal) × Tmission

with practical bounds of 56–100 days to avoid pathological bins. Bright, variable sources receive short bins that resolve rapid flares; faint sources get longer bins that preserve significance. With this I generated optimally binned light curves for all neutrino-candidate blazars and used them to build SEDs and examine short-term variability.

The project gave me experience with Fermi-LAT catalogues and TS statistics, multi-wavelength SED assembly, and how sampling choices affect variability detection. SED interpretation has since become central to my master’s thesis on LBG SED modelling.
