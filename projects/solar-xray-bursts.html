Detecting and classifying solar X-ray bursts

A peak-detection and clustering pipeline for noisy Chandrayaan-2 solar X-ray light curves, built for an ISRO challenge.

At the Inter IIT Technical Meet, a national competition between the IITs, our team of five took on the Astronomy & Astrophysics challenge: an ISRO problem statement to build a web application that detects peaks in solar X-ray light curves and extracts their physical features.

We had about 700 light curves from the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter, all very noisy. Gaussian smoothing suppressed spurious fluctuations without destroying burst structure. We then defined a burst formally, by prominence, width, height above background and neighbouring behaviour, and built a peak-detection routine with scipy.signal that identified clear bursts in about 400 light curves, holding the rest out for validation.

For each detected event we extracted rise time, decay time, duration and peak flux, and clustered events with k-means by temporal structure and relative intensity. This formed the back end of the web-based classification tool.

The model struggled with overlapping peaks and low-signal events, but the project gave me hands-on experience with time-series analysis, signal processing, feature engineering, clustering, and the practical difficulties of applying machine learning to real spacecraft data as part of a fast-moving team.
